# RoundGuard theorem ledger and complete mathematical proofs

Independent review, 2026-10-03. This file states the precise mathematical scope rather than a novelty claim. `PROVED` means a complete ordinary mathematical proof is supplied below. No theorem here is proof-assistant checked; none is labelled `MACHINE-CHECKED`. Finite executable checks, when recorded separately, are only sanity checks.

## 1. Semantics and notation

Inputs are integer ticks x=(x_1,...,x_d) in Z^d. Constants and scaling factors are exact rational numbers. Expressions are finite trees (or acyclic graphs) generated by

    e ::= c | x_i | e+e | e-e | r*e | Q(q,m,e),

where c,r are rational, q is a strictly positive rational, and m is FLOOR, CEILING, or HALF_EVEN. There is no floating-point evaluation in this semantics.

A physical input with fixed rational tick quantum delta_i is represented by delta_i*x_i; all erased coefficients and periods are therefore computed with respect to integer ticks, as the implementation's `affine` function does. The quantizers are defined by

    Q(q,FLOOR,y)   = q*floor(y/q),
    Q(q,CEILING,y) = q*ceil(y/q),
    Q(q,HALF_EVEN,y) = q*he(y/q),

where he chooses a nearest integer, breaking a tie by choosing the even integer. Thus he(-1/2)=0, he(1/2)=0, he(3/2)=2, and he(-3/2)=-2. A grid is anchored at zero.

Erasure replaces every quantizer by its child. The resulting affine function is

    bar(e)(x) = b_e + sum_i a_e,i*x_i.

For a rational r in lowest terms, den(r) is its positive denominator, with den(0)=1. The lcm of an empty collection is 1. Every box below is a nonempty box with finite integer endpoints L_i <= U_i. A rounding-placement family is a finite declared set of expressions; completeness is always relative to that declared set.

## 2. Quantizer translation and parity

**Lemma 1 — PROVED.** For all real y, integer k, and positive q,

    Q(q,FLOOR,y+kq)   = Q(q,FLOOR,y)+kq,
    Q(q,CEILING,y+kq) = Q(q,CEILING,y)+kq,
    Q(q,HALF_EVEN,y+2kq) = Q(q,HALF_EVEN,y)+2kq.

**Proof.** Adding an integer k translates the defining inequalities for floor and ceiling by k, proving the first two identities. Adding 2k translates the set of nearest integers by 2k and preserves the parity of each candidate. Therefore the selected candidate also translates by 2k, proving the third identity. Multiplying each normalized identity by q proves the statements. This also covers negative k. QED.

The factor two is essential without additional assumptions. With q=1, he(1/2)=0 but he(3/2)=2, so translation by one tick fails. The safe period below need not be a *minimal* period: he(x)=x on integer inputs, although its syntax-derived period is two.

## 3. A computable shift certificate

Let E be a finite collection of expressions in the grammar. For each coordinate i, inspect every quantizer node v=Q(q_v,m_v,u_v) in their union and define

    T_i = lcm_v den(a_u_v,i / (p_v*q_v)),
    p_v = 1 for FLOOR or CEILING, and p_v = 2 for HALF_EVEN.

The denominator is computed after reduction. This precise formula is not always the same as doubling den(a_u_v,i/q_v): for example, a_u_v,i/q_v=2/3 already has an even numerator and requires denominator 3 after division by two.

**Theorem 2 — PROVED.** Every subexpression e in E satisfies, for every x in Z^d and coordinate i,

    e(x+T_i*unit_i) = e(x)+a_e,i*T_i.

More generally, for every k in Z^d,

    e(x+(k_1*T_1,...,k_d*T_d))
      = e(x)+sum_i a_e,i*k_i*T_i.

**Proof.** Fix i and apply structural induction. A constant has coefficient zero and is unchanged. A variable x_j shifts by T_i if j=i and is unchanged otherwise, exactly its erased-coefficient drift. Addition and subtraction add and subtract the established child drifts. Multiplication by a rational r multiplies both the child drift and erased coefficient by r.

At a quantizer Q(q,m,u), induction says the child shifts by a_u,i*T_i. Because T_i is a multiple of den(a_u,i/(p*q)), the number a_u,i*T_i/(p*q) is an integer. The child drift is therefore an integer multiple of q for FLOOR or CEILING, and an even integer multiple of q for HALF_EVEN. Lemma 1 gives exactly the same output drift. Erasure leaves the child's coefficient unchanged at a quantizer, so this is a_e,i*T_i. This completes structural induction.

Iterate the positive-shift identity for nonnegative k_i. For a negative step, apply the positive-shift identity at x-T_i*unit_i and rearrange. Applying the resulting identities one coordinate at a time proves the vector identity. QED.

**Corollary 3 — PROVED.** The quantization error e-bar(e) is periodic with coordinate periods T_i. If expressions u and v have the same erased slope vector (their erased constants may differ), u-v is periodic with the same periods.

**Proof.** The erased affine function has precisely the drift in Theorem 2. Subtract its identity, or subtract the identities of u and v with equal slopes. QED.

Identical full erasure is sufficient but stronger than necessary. Equality of erased slopes is the exact stated sufficient condition. Unequal slopes invalidate the periodic-difference conclusion in general: x and 0 have difference x.

## 4. Exact reduction of a bounded input box

Write w_i=U_i-L_i+1, h_i=min(T_i,w_i), and

    H = product_i {L_i,...,L_i+h_i-1}.

For x in the original box X, define

    pi_i(x_i) = L_i + ((x_i-L_i) mod T_i),

where mod has its nonnegative Euclidean meaning.

**Theorem 4 — PROVED.** Let f be any scalar-valued function on Z^d periodic with coordinate periods T_i. Then pi maps X into H, f(x)=f(pi(x)) for all x in X, and

    {f(x):x in X} = {f(h):h in H}.

Consequently equality of two common-slope variants, their exact signed discrepancy extrema, and their exact maximum absolute discrepancy on X can all be decided or computed on H. Every witness in H is a genuine witness in X.

**Proof.** If w_i<T_i, every x_i-L_i lies in [0,w_i-1], so the remainder is x_i-L_i and pi_i(x_i)=x_i. If w_i>=T_i, the remainder lies in [0,T_i-1], so pi_i(x_i) lies in the stated reduced interval. Thus pi(x) is in H. Each coordinate difference x_i-pi_i(x_i) is a multiple of T_i. Periodicity applied one coordinate at a time gives f(x)=f(pi(x)). Hence the value set on X is contained in the one on H. Conversely H is a subset of X, yielding the reverse containment. Finite value-set equality proves every extrema statement. The witness statement follows directly from H subset X. QED.

For a finite family with a common erased slope, one can apply the theorem simultaneously to all pairwise differences, or to the family range max_v e_v(x)-min_v e_v(x). Evaluation signatures on H exactly identify equivalence classes. The proof does not assert efficient enumeration: |H| can still be exponential or larger in the input description, and the syntax-derived T_i can be very conservative.

## 5. Exact residue-extrema DP for independent affine lines

This section gives a specialized algorithm that avoids enumerating the Cartesian product of line domains. It does not apply automatically to arbitrary expression graphs.

Assume n>=1 independent integer inputs x_i in [L_i,U_i]. Their exact normalized line values are

    y_i = a_i*x_i+c_i,

with rational a_i,c_i. The common rounding grid is normalized to one. For an original grid q, replace y_i by y_i/q and multiply the eventual monetary discrepancy by q.

Choose a positive integer B clearing every denominator of a_i and c_i. Set

    A_i=B*a_i in Z, C_i=B*c_i in Z, z_i=A_i*x_i+C_i=B*y_i,
    M=2B.

For each i, a nonempty finite set K_i of allowed independent local operations is declared. Each operation is FLOOR, CEILING, HALF_EVEN, or IDENTITY. A fixed program has singleton K_i. Optional local round/no-round choices are represented by independent K_i; an arbitrary correlated family is outside this theorem. Let R_i be a selected operation, R_0 a fixed outer FLOOR, CEILING, or HALF_EVEN operation, and define

    P_total(x)=R_0(sum_i y_i),
    P_lines(x,R)=sum_i R_i(y_i),
    Delta=P_total-P_lines.

Define each local error d_i=B*R_i(y_i)-z_i. It is an integer, including d_i=0 for IDENTITY. Define the normalized outer-error function

    C_0(r)=B*R_0(r/B)-r for r in {0,...,M-1}.

**Lemma 5 — PROVED.** Let

    T_i=M/gcd(|A_i|,M)=den(a_i/2),

using gcd(0,M)=M. The local pair (z_i mod M,d_i) is invariant under x_i -> x_i+T_i for every allowed local operation. All attainable local pairs on [L_i,U_i] and their in-domain witnesses can therefore be enumerated on the first min(T_i,U_i-L_i+1) ticks. In particular, T_i<=M.

**Proof.** Put g=gcd(|A_i|,M). Then A_i*T_i=M*(A_i/g), an integer multiple of M, proving residue invariance. The normalized line-value shift is A_i*T_i/B=2*A_i/g, an even integer. Lemma 1, or the identity operation, translates its local result by exactly this shift. Multiplying by B and subtracting the translated z_i shows that d_i is unchanged. The bounded representative argument in Theorem 4, in one coordinate, proves completeness and witness validity. Since gcd(|A_i|,M)>=1, T_i<=M. Finally den(a_i/2)=den(A_i/(2B))=M/g, including A_i=0. QED.

For each residue r attainable locally, let ell_i(r) and h_i(r) be the minimum and maximum d_i among its local inputs and choices. Store a local witness (x_i,R_i) attaining each endpoint. Residues absent locally are marked unreachable, not assigned finite endpoints. These extrema do not assert that all integer values between them are attainable.

Define V_k^-(r) and V_k^+(r) as the minimum and maximum total local error D=sum_{i<=k}d_i over the first k lines subject to sum_{i<=k}z_i congruent to r modulo M. Initialize only residue zero as reachable, with both values zero, and use

    V_k^-(r)=min_{s+t congruent r (mod M)} (V_{k-1}^-(s)+ell_k(t)),
    V_k^+(r)=max_{s+t congruent r (mod M)} (V_{k-1}^+(s)+h_k(t)),

where only reachable states participate.

**Theorem 6 — PROVED.** The recurrence computes the exact feasible extrema at every prefix. The complete signed discrepancy extrema are

    Delta_min = min_reachable_r (C_0(r)-V_n^+(r))/B,
    Delta_max = max_reachable_r (C_0(r)-V_n^-(r))/B.

The exact maximum absolute discrepancy is max(-Delta_min,Delta_max). The two programs (or the declared product family of choices) are equivalent on the entire input box exactly when Delta_min=Delta_max=0. Backpointers reconstruct an in-domain input and an allowed operation assignment attaining either extreme.

**Proof.** For k=0 the empty assignment has residue zero and error zero, and no other state is feasible. Suppose the stated meaning is established at k-1. Every feasible k-line assignment decomposes into an independently feasible prefix with residue s and the kth local input/operation with residue t; its total residue is s+t modulo M and its error is the sum of their errors. Conversely every such pair combines into a feasible k-line assignment because inputs and operation choices are independent. At fixed s,t, the least additive error is the sum of the two least errors, and is attained by their stored witnesses. The greatest error is the sum of their greatest errors, also attained. Taking the minimum or maximum over all residue decompositions proves the recurrence and its attainability by induction.

For a complete assignment, put S=sum_i z_i, D=sum_i d_i, and r=S mod M. Then

    B*Delta = B*R_0(S/B)-(S+D)
            = (B*R_0(S/B)-S)-D.

Because S=r+M*j for an integer j, the argument S/B differs from r/B by 2j. Lemma 1 implies B*R_0(S/B)-S=C_0(r), including negative S. At fixed r the last display is C_0(r)-D, a decreasing affine function of D. Its least and greatest feasible values therefore use exactly V_n^+(r) and V_n^-(r), respectively. Taking extrema over reachable r proves both formulas and attainability. Every real number between the global extrema has absolute value at most max(-Delta_min,Delta_max); one of the attained endpoints achieves that bound. A set of discrepancies is identically zero exactly when both attained extrema are zero.

For each recurrence endpoint, store its predecessor residue and the local endpoint witness used to achieve it. Starting at the final residue selected by the appropriate extrema formula and following predecessors recovers each local input/operation. The initial state must be zero by induction, and every local input was chosen in the reduced interval, which is a subset of its original interval. All choices belong to their declared K_i. The reconstructed assignment thus attains the claimed result and is valid in the original box and product family. QED.

**Complexity — PROVED bound on the stated direct implementation.** With at most four local options per line, local enumeration costs O(nM) arithmetic operations. Direct dense recurrence evaluation costs O(nM^2) operations and O(M) working value storage. Backpointer recovery uses O(nM) additional records. More precisely, a sparse update costs O(sum_i M*|R_i|), where R_i is the local attainable residue set. Preprocessing gcd/lcm and modular arithmetic depend on the binary sizes of the input coefficients and endpoints. Errors have magnitude at most nB in this semantics, so DP-value bit lengths are O(log(nB)); witness tick values retain the input endpoint bit lengths. These are arithmetic-operation bounds and are pseudo-polynomial in B. They are not polynomial-time bounds in the binary encoding of B. If all local and outer operations are FLOOR, CEILING, or IDENTITY, the proof also works with M=B and T_i=B/gcd(|A_i|,B).

The state factor two cannot be dropped blindly with HALF_EVEN. Let n=1, y=x+1/2, x in {0,1}, local FLOOR, and outer HALF_EVEN. With B=2, both inputs have z mod B=1 and d=-1, yet the total-minus-lines discrepancies are 0 and 1. Modulus 2B distinguishes them.

### Certified nonnegative extension

**Lemma 6a — PROVED.** For t>=0, conventional HALF_UP (ties away from zero) equals floor(t+1/2), DOWN (toward zero) equals floor(t), and UP (away from zero) equals ceil(t). Consequently, if t,t+j are both nonnegative and j is integer, each of these three operations translates its result by j.

**Proof.** On the nonnegative half-line, a halfway tie away from zero chooses the larger nearest integer, exactly floor(t+1/2). Truncation toward zero is floor and truncation away from zero is ceiling, including t=0. Applying the integer-translation identities for floor and ceiling proves the last statement. Both endpoints being nonnegative is required for these identifications. QED.

**Theorem 6b — PROVED.** The residue-extrema DP, witness theorem, and complexity bound in Section 5 remain exact when local/outer operations additionally include HALF_UP, DOWN, and UP, provided every exact normalized raw affine line is nonnegative throughout its original input interval. A sufficient and exact check is

    min(a_i*L_i+c_i, a_i*U_i+c_i) >= 0

for each line, and c_i>=0 for every constant line. This is a domain-specific certificate and does not authorize generic signed-domain period reduction for these modes.

**Proof.** A scalar affine function attains its minimum on an interval at an endpoint, so the stated check proves every raw line value in the original domain is nonnegative, even when a slope or input tick is negative. Its reduced representative interval is a subset of its original interval, so every representative line value is also nonnegative. Their sums are nonnegative. For each line, its actual argument and representative argument differ by an even integer by Lemma 5; both are nonnegative. The original three operations preserve errors by Lemma 1, and the new three do so by Lemma 6a. IDENTITY also preserves its zero error. Hence every local pair and witness reduction remains complete.

For every actual assignment, S=sum z_i is nonnegative; its Euclidean residue r=S mod (2B) is nonnegative as well. Thus both outer arguments S/B and r/B are nonnegative and differ by an even integer. Lemma 6a supplies the outer-correction identity for the new modes, while Lemma 1 supplies it for the original modes. The recurrence depends only on additive errors and independent choices, so its induction and terminal formulas are unchanged. Backpointer witnesses remain in the original box, where the sign certificate holds. No state or recurrence size changes, proving the same complexity bound. QED.

Requiring every raw line to be nonnegative is a simple sufficient certificate for this extension; it is not asserted to be necessary. A mixed-sign line family whose total happens to be nonnegative does not meet the certificate. Signed unsupported modes must therefore use the unreduced bounded fallback unless separately certified.

## 6. Sharp elementary aggregation bounds

Here operands y_j are arbitrary rational values, not constrained to a particular application's input box. These lemmas are elementary facts, not claims of originality.

**Lemma 7 — PROVED.** For n>=1 and a shared grid q,

    -(n-1)q <= sum_j Q(q,FLOOR,y_j)-Q(q,FLOOR,sum_j y_j) <= 0,
    0 <= sum_j Q(q,CEILING,y_j)-Q(q,CEILING,sum_j y_j) <= (n-1)q.

Both endpoint bounds are attained on rational operands.

**Proof.** Normalize q=1 and write y_j=k_j+f_j with k_j integer and 0<=f_j<1. The floor discrepancy is -floor(sum_j f_j), which is an integer in [-(n-1),0]. Zero is attained with all operands zero. For n>=2, choosing all f_j=1-1/(2n) gives sum f_j=n-1/2 and discrepancy -(n-1). For n=1 the expression is identically zero. Since ceil(t)=-floor(-t), replacing all operands by their negatives gives the ceiling statement and witnesses. Rescale by q. QED.

**Lemma 8 — PROVED.** Let beta_n=he(n/2). Equivalently,

    beta_n = n/2                     if n is even,
             (n-1)/2                if n congruent to 1 modulo 4,
             (n+1)/2                if n congruent to 3 modulo 4.

For n>=1,

    |sum_j Q(q,HALF_EVEN,y_j)-Q(q,HALF_EVEN,sum_j y_j)| <= q*beta_n.

The bound is sharp: y_j=q/2 for every j attains the negative endpoint, and y_j=-q/2 for every j attains the positive endpoint. In particular the sharp bound for n=5 is 2q, not 3q.

**Proof.** Normalize q=1. Set k_j=he(y_j) and f_j=y_j-k_j. Each f_j is in [-1/2,1/2]. If |f_j|=1/2, tie-to-even implies k_j is even. Let K=sum k_j and F=sum f_j. The discrepancy is K-he(K+F).

For n=2h, |F|<=h. Rounding a value in [K-h,K+h] to nearest cannot select an integer outside that interval, since the integer endpoint is strictly nearer than any integer past it. Therefore the absolute discrepancy is at most h.

For n=2h+1, |F|<=h+1/2. In the strict interior -h-1/2<F<h+1/2, nearest rounding selects an integer from K-h through K+h. At F=h+1/2 all f_j must equal 1/2, so all k_j and hence K are even. The tie selects K+h when h is even, and K+h+1 when h is odd. The lower endpoint F=-h-1/2 likewise forces K even and selects K-h when h is even, and K-h-1 when h is odd. Hence the largest absolute discrepancy is h+(h mod 2), exactly beta_n. The constant operands 1/2 give K=0 and F=n/2, attaining discrepancy -he(n/2). Negating these operands reverses the discrepancy, because nearest-even rounding is odd: the nearest candidate set and its even member negate. Rescale by q. QED.

The positive endpoint is also attainable using nonnegative operands y_j=3q/2 for every j: all local outputs are 2q, their sum is 2nq, and even-integer translation plus oddness of he gives he(3n/2)=2n-he(n/2). Thus nonnegative operands alone already attain both sharp endpoints when unconstrained by an application box.

These universal bounds need not be attainable on a restricted application domain. The residue DP computes the actual attainable extrema instead.

## 7. Sound compositional error envelopes

**Lemma 9 — PROVED.** A closed interval [l_e,h_e] enclosing e-bar(e) is computed by assigning [0,0] to constants/variables, adding interval endpoints for addition, using [l_u-h_v,h_u-l_v] for subtraction, scaling and swapping endpoints when the scale is negative, and adding [-q,0], [0,q], or [-q/2,q/2] at FLOOR, CEILING, or HALF_EVEN nodes, respectively.

**Proof.** Addition, subtraction, and scaling preserve the indicated interval containments by elementary inequalities. At a quantizer,

    Q(q,m,u)-bar(u)=(u-bar(u))+(Q(q,m,u)-u).

The second summand lies in (-q,0] for FLOOR, [0,q) for CEILING, and [-q/2,q/2] for HALF_EVEN. Their displayed closed envelopes contain these exact sets. Induction proves the claim. QED.

This is a sound enclosure; it does not preserve correlations between shared subexpressions and is not generally tight. It provides no exact global worst-case certificate without the finite reduction/DP or another exact method.

## 8. Exact integer-logic encoding

**Lemma 10 — PROVED.** Every expression in the grammar admits an exact existential integer-linear encoding. Equivalence counterexamples on an integer box and signed-discrepancy threshold queries can be expressed in Presburger arithmetic after clearing constant denominators.

**Proof.** Inductively choose a positive integer D_e such that every value of e has numerator n_e in Z when written n_e/D_e: use constant denominators, 1 for variables, an lcm for addition/subtraction, the product with the rational scale's denominator for scaling, and den(q) for a quantizer output q*k. Thus every intermediate node has a fixed rational denominator.

At a quantizer, write the normalized child value z=u/q=N/D with D>0 and integer N, using its fixed denominator and rational q. FLOOR is encoded by D*k<=N<D*(k+1); CEILING by D*(k-1)<N<=D*k. HALF_EVEN is encoded by

    D*(2k-1) < 2N < D*(2k+1),
    or [2N = D*(2k-1) and k even],
    or [2N = D*(2k+1) and k even].

The even condition is k=2t for an integer t. Exactly one k satisfies the appropriate mode relation, by the semantic definitions. The output equation relates its fixed-denominator numerator to q*k by clearing positive constant denominators. Every non-rounding node likewise uses a linear equation with constant coefficients. Adding all node relations yields an existential integer formula whose models correspond exactly to semantic evaluations. Input bounds are linear. A nonzero pair difference or a rational threshold is another linear relation after multiplying by positive denominators. QED.

This is an encoding proof, not a claim that generic Presburger decidability is new, that a specific SMT implementation is verified, or that timeout/unknown establishes any theorem.

## 9. Explicit exclusions and safe extensions

**Counterexamples — exact calculations.** On a signed domain, the standard HALF_UP convention of ties away from zero, DOWN toward zero, and UP away from zero lack the global translation property required by Theorem 2. For any positive integer k, use y=-1/2, q=1, and shift k. HALF_UP gives -1 at y and k at y+k, instead of k-1. DOWN gives 0 at y and k-1 at y+k, instead of k. UP gives -1 at y and k at y+k, instead of k-1. Even integer shifts do not repair these crossings. Theorem 6b proves a separate nonnegative affine-line DP certificate; it does not extend the generic grammar's signed-domain period certificate.

Nonlinear multiplication, variable grids/divisors, division by an input, saturation, ordinary threshold guards, arbitrary min/max, and loops are outside the core theorem. For example max(x,0) cannot have one affine drift under a nonzero translation over all signed integer inputs. A min/max extension *is* sound if every compared child has the same certified drift: min(u+c,v+c)=min(u,v)+c, and similarly for max. A conditional extension needs an invariant guard and identically drifting branches. These are sufficient local extension conditions, not an automatic certificate for every branching program.

For unsupported modes/constructs, a bounded exact SMT encoding can remain useful when the constructs are encodable, but that route must not reuse an invalid reduced domain. Non-Cartesian input constraints also cannot be discarded: independent representative reduction may leave the feasible set, and the specialized DP relies on independence.
